# Editorial Critique

Append `## Editorial Critique` after a horizontal rule, keeping the speaker's article unchanged. Focus on issues that materially affect understanding or correctness; avoid reviewing every sentence. If none are found, state that briefly, identifying whether external verification was performed.

Keep three activities distinct, using subsections when needed:

1. **Internal analysis:** Evaluate contradictions, unsupported assumptions, questionable causal relationships, logical gaps, invalid inferences, overgeneralizations, missing qualifications, and misleading terminology from the transcript itself.
2. **External fact verification:** Only when enabled, check significant factual concerns using reliable sources, prioritizing primary evidence. Cite direct supporting links for corrections, distinguish findings from interpretations, and account for the presentation's publication date when assessing time-sensitive information. If tools or evidence are unavailable, disclose the limit; never imply verification occurred. Do not automatically verify every factual statement.
3. **Transcription ambiguity review:** Record consequential uncertain readings and moderate-confidence corrections that materially change a claim, technical explanation, or argument. Distinguish them from mistakes made by the speaker.

For each significant factual or reasoning issue, provide the claim, source timestamp or identifiable location, concern, supported correction or alternative if available, and uncertainty/verification status. Use these classifications accurately:

- **Confirmed error:** Demonstrable from available source material or reliable external evidence. With external verification disabled, the source material itself must demonstrate the error.
- **Internal inconsistency:** Conflicting statements within the source; identify both locations where available.
- **Potential issue:** A plausible concern requiring further verification.
- **Unverified claim:** An externally checkable claim for which sufficient evidence is unavailable.

Do not equate absence of evidence in a presentation with falsity. An ambiguity note may simply give competing readings and their consequences without pretending it is a factual-error classification. Retain the speaker's original uncertainty, conclusions, gaps, and errors in the article; put supported corrections here only.
